# Purpose

This document defines the content strategy, copywriting standards, writing tone, messaging principles, and presentation guidelines for the LeadFlow Software Proposal.

The objective is to ensure the proposal reads like it was written by a product marketing team at a premium SaaS company (such as Stripe, Linear, Notion, Vercel, or HubSpot). It must communicate business value clearly to decision-makers and avoid looking like software documentation or a PRD.

---

# Writing Principles

Every page and section in the proposal must adhere to these core principles:

- **Value-First:** Focus on the business outcomes (time saved, increased visibility, team accountability) before describing the technology.
- **Visual Balance:** Every page should aim for roughly 40% text and 60% visuals (including cards, diagrams, tables, and mockup placeholders).
- **Skim-Friendliness:** A busy decision-maker should be able to scan any page and understand its core takeaway within 15 seconds.
- **No Fluff:** Avoid corporate buzzwords and marketing exaggerations. Build trust through clarity and concrete descriptions.

---

# Tone of Voice

The LeadFlow tone must always remain:

- **Professional:** Confident and authoritative without sounding defensive or clinical.
- **Clear:** Direct, using active verbs and avoiding passive structures.
- **Friendly & Solution-Oriented:** Position the proposal as a partnership rather than a transaction.
- **Honest:** Set realistic boundaries regarding project scope, features, and future phases.

### Tone Examples

* **Preferred:** "We simplify recruitment operations by centralizing lead management and reporting."
* **Avoid:** "Our revolutionary AI-powered platform completely transforms recruitment forever."

---

# Proposal Narrative

The proposal follows this streamlined storytelling sequence representing the actual approved document structure:

1. **Cover Page:** Introduce LeadFlow, branding, client and metadata.
2. **Executive Summary:** Highlight current problems, the LeadFlow solution, and the workflow transformation.
3. **Current Challenges:** Detail pain points (Manual Excel, WhatsApp Reporting, duplicate leads, etc.) in a 3x3 grid.
4. **Why Change?:** Present the value proposition of centralizing operations.
5. **Introducing LeadFlow:** Introduce the PWA platform concept and security with the Login mockup.
6. **Manager Experience:** Present the desktop Manager Dashboard mockup for live recruitment oversight.
7. **Intern Experience:** Present the Intern Dashboard workspace mockup showing daily target checking and queue tables.
8. **Lead Database:** Present the Lead Management Table mockup showing filtering, CSV import, and distribution actions.
9. **Executive Analytics:** Present the Analytics Dashboard mockup showing conversion funnels, donuts, and top performing lists.
10. **Mobile Adaptability:** Present the Smartphone Mockup with features callouts.
11. **Implementation Roadmap:** Present the 6-stage rollout timeline (Discovery to Support).
12. **Commercial Model:** Present pricing, setups, and outcome-focused value.
13. **Return on Investment:** Present a side-by-side workflow comparison and checklist.
14. **Long-Term Support:** Present the partnership lifecycle timeline and support plans.
15. **Why LeadFlow Summary:** Present the comparative summary (current operations vs. LeadFlow).
16. **Launch Timeline:** Present the next steps 4-stage rollout kickoff timeline.
17. **Contact Close:** Present the founder/developer contact card close.

# Content Hierarchy

Every page in the proposal must follow a uniform content layout:

```
Hero Title (Large Page Title)

↓

One-sentence description (explains the page in 1–2 lines)

↓

Primary Content (prefer cards, comparisons, or lists over long paragraphs)

↓

One Dominant Visual (Mockup, Diagram, or Timeline)

↓

Key Takeaway (a highlighted callout summarizing the page value)
```

# Business Language

Explain technology through the lens of business benefits.

### Terminology Lock-In
Always use the following terms consistently:
- **Lead:** Relates directly to database records. Do not use "Prospect" or "Contact" interchangeably. Note that "Candidate" is permitted when describing the end-person being called in communication or queue mockups (e.g., "Call Candidate").
- **Intern:** Relates directly to recruiters/interns. Never use "Agent" or "User" in descriptions.
- **Manager:** Relates directly to managers. Never use "Administrator" or "Admin" in descriptions.
- **Batch:** Always refers to imported groups of leads.

### Benefit-First Writing
* **Instead of:** "React and Supabase provide real-time synchronization."
* **Write:** "Managers and interns see recruitment updates instantly without refreshing their screens."

# Copywriting Standards

### Sentence and Paragraph Length
- **Paragraph Limit:** Maximum 3 lines per paragraph.
- **Sentence Limit:** Maximum 20 words per sentence. If longer, split it.
- **Bullet Lists:** Use 3 to 6 bullet points. Each bullet should contain exactly one complete idea.

### Grammar & Voice
- **Active Voice:** Always use active voice.
  * *Active:* "Managers assign leads to interns."
  * *Passive:* "Leads are assigned by managers to interns."

### Word Choice Rules

**Preferred Words:**
* Centralize, Manage, Track, Monitor, Assign, Review, Analyze, Simplify, Organize, Support, Scale, Optimize, Automate.

**Banned Words:**
* Revolutionary, Game-changing, Cutting-edge, Groundbreaking, Magic, Perfect, Ultimate, World-class, Best-in-class, Unlimited.

# Quality Checklist

Before finalizing copy, verify:
* [ ] The page has one primary objective.
* [ ] It starts with a strong headline and a 1–2 line introduction.
* [ ] No paragraph is longer than 3 lines.
* [ ] Terminology is aligned (Lead, Intern, Manager, Batch).
* [ ] Tone is helpful and professional, containing no banned hype words.
* [ ] Active voice is used throughout.
* [ ] Includes one key takeaway callout.
